Summary
The solution gives a yes-instance and a no-instance with figures to illustrate both cases. For the complexity part, it argues that the problem is in Σ_2^P by expressing it in the form “there exists a coloring such that for all subgraphs isomorphic to G, the subgraph is not monochromatic.” It explains this using an existential step for guessing the coloring and a universal step over subgraphs, and connects this to NP and co-NP.

Does it answer the question?
Yes, it answers both parts. It gives valid examples for the yes and no cases, and it clearly places the problem in the second level of the polynomial hierarchy. The structure of the argument matches what the question asks for.

Parts that are confusing or unclear
The solution is quite formal and symbol-heavy, which makes it feel technical, but it was a bit harder for me to follow in some places. For example, the step where the universal quantifier is converted into a co-NP statement moves quickly, and I had to reread it to understand the logic. A slightly more informal explanation in words of what the quantifiers mean in plain language might help. It might help to briefly explain that it works because F simply does not contain G at all.

Are there parts that are wrong?
I did not notice any clear logical errors. The quantifier structure seems correct, and the reasoning about NP and co-NP under the given assumption looks consistent.

Parts that are ambiguous or imprecise
The expression Σ_2^P = NP^co-NP is written a bit compactly. It might help to briefly clarify what that notation means in words, since not everyone may immediately recognize it.
Overall, the solution is thorough and well structured, and the figures are helpful. A little more plain-language explanation alongside the formal notation would make it easier to follow, atleast for me personally.
