## Importance of Context in Case Studies

- Context shapes phenomena; cannot isolate variables easily
- Medical example: neck growth, multiple possible causes (allergy, sting, diet)
- Coding example: security depends on surrounding system, not just code line
- Emphasizes holistic view over single‑factor analysis

## Conducting a Case Study

- Start with a clear research question (how/why, not how‑much)
- Ensure question can be answered with a case study & linked to theory
- Collect observations, data, and relevant variables
- Analyze results, compare to model predictions, discuss limitations

## Example: Medical Case Study

- Patient presents with neck growth, vague symptoms
- Diagnostic path: describe, summarize, explore possible causes
- Highlight many variables (environment, diet, injuries)
- Stress that treatment decisions depend on full contextual picture

## Example: Apache Incubator Sustainability Forecast

- Forecast graduation likelihood for incubating projects
- Analyzed three projects (IDs 8203, 8256, etc.) out of \~300
- Identified downturns linked to low email/commit activity
- Model predicts early warning; communication lapses (missing reports) trigger risk
- Suggested proactive email reminders to project‑management committee

## Modeling vs. Case Study Insights

- Models give starting point; case studies reveal real‑world deviations
- Case studies can validate, refine, or challenge model assumptions
- Avoid treating case studies as mere anecdotes; tie back to theory

## Tools & Recommendations

- Consider LLM‑assisted analysis for large‑scale case study data
- Use automated prompts to flag at‑risk projects early
- Combine quantitative forecasts with qualitative case insights for better decisions
