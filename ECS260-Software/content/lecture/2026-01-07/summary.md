## Research Ethics & Plagiarism

- Grading based on quality of work, not on how “interesting” the topic seems
- Avoid attaching personal feelings to results; can lead to anxiety or depression
- Copying another’s work (even a small portion) can trigger suspension; &lt; ½ page is risky
- Proper attribution required; using others’ code or text without credit is prohibited

## Use of LLMs

- LLMs (e.g., Gemini, ChatGPT) can be used to brainstorm, suggest ideas, or generate outliers
- Do **not** use LLMs to write whole sections or produce final text
- Treat LLM output as a starting point; verify and rewrite in your own words

## Research Methods Overview

- Two broad categories: **lab (controlled)** vs **field (in‑the‑wild)**
- Lab: precise, rigid experiments, single variables, high reproducibility
- Field: rich, descriptive, captures real‑world complexity, less control
- Methods can be plotted on a precision‑richness spectrum; both have value

## Data Sources & Mining

- GitHub archives provide large‑scale project data for hypothesis testing
- Can compare metrics across languages (e.g., Java vs Python bug rates)
- Combine artifact analysis with controlled experiments for robust findings

## Designing a Study

- **Theory**: testable, falsifiable statements about the world; must be disproved to be useful
- **Hypothesis**: derived from theory; specific, measurable prediction
- Identify unit of analysis (line of code, project, ecosystem) and target population
- Plan sampling strategy; consider threats to validity (confounding factors, bias)
- For surveys, obtain IRB approval and recruit participants appropriately

## Grading & Evaluation

- Assessment based on the quality and completeness of the research deliverable
- No penalty for “interesting” topics; focus on rigorous methodology and clear results
- Mental health reminder: don’t let results affect self‑worth; keep perspective

## Advice & Mental Health

- Resist the impulse to dive in without planning; take time to outline the whole project
- Collaborate early to avoid “one‑person bottlenecks” in data collection or analysis
- Keep a balanced view of success; research is a collective effort, not a solo race
