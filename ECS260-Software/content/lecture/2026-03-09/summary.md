## Interview Methodology

- Interviews vs. surveys: static vs. interactive, allow deeper probing
- Types: exploratory, structured, in‑depth; choose based on participant role
- Goal: capture participants’ experiences, motivations, and contextual details
- Use interview data to refine hypotheses, validate findings, and uncover processes

## Practical Tips for Conducting Interviews

- Create a flexible guide: topic headings + optional probe questions
- Start with open‑ended questions, follow up with specific probes as needed
- Avoid leading or double‑barreled questions (e.g., “Why join open source and does it improve productivity?”)
- Use silence or pauses to encourage elaboration
- Be ready to shift topics if interviewee gets uncomfortable or off‑track

## Considerations & Bias

- Watch for potential bias: leading questions, social desirability, privacy concerns
- Recruit participants ethically – obtain permission, respect gatekeepers (e.g., department chairs)
- Keep interview length realistic for participants (plan for 30‑45 min)
- Document and anonymize responses to protect confidentiality

## General Reminders

- Surveys are static; interviews let you adapt in real time
- Interview data can dramatically shorten research cycles (weeks → minutes)
- Flexibility and active listening are key to successful interviews
- Keep track of time, notes, and any technical issues during sessions
