## Key Concepts

- Audience background shapes how memorable a lecture is
- Communication precision = gap between intended vs. interpreted message
- Storytelling provides structure, boosts recall & engagement
- Settings & tools (slides, audio quality) affect message delivery

## Storytelling Benefits

- Turns raw data into a narrative flow (left‑side story vs. right‑side bullet list)
- Helps audience retain take‑aways (e.g., tortoise‑and‑hare moral)
- Works across media: paintings, comics, manga, movies, charts

## Historical Examples

- Prehistoric cave paintings (\~30,000 yr ago) depict animal migrations
- Narrative elements in ancient boat scenes (weapon, shark, rescue)
- Comic‑style panels infer story from setting, characters, expressions

## Data Storytelling Process

1. Data exploration → collect insights
2. Organize insights into a coherent narrative (choose genre & structure)
3. Design visual presentation (slides, scrolling, drill‑down, etc.)
4. Present, gather feedback, iterate

## Genres & Formats

- **Magazines / News articles** – linear, text‑heavy
- **Annotated charts** – visual + explanatory notes (e.g., Ukraine‑Russia casualties)
- **Scrolling stories** – vertical flow, continuous reveal
- **Drill‑down interfaces** – user‑driven exploration of detailed data

## Narrative Structures

- **Author‑driven** – fixed sequence, strong messaging, limited interaction
- **Reader‑driven** – open‑ended, high interactivity, exploratory
- **Hybrid (Martini glass)** – guided narrative followed by optional exploration

## Design Techniques

- **Reverse bar charts** – color & orientation cue emotions (e.g., casualty trends)
- **Perception cues** – visual hierarchy (large element top‑left draws eye)
- **Highlighting** – gray‑out irrelevant items, accentuate focus points
- **Annotations** – mark significant events on line charts (inflation, unemployment)
- Consistent color palettes to maintain visual continuity

## Common Challenges

- Maintaining a single, clear message vs. information dump
- Ensuring all visuals stay connected to the narrative thread
- Selecting appropriate visual elements for the communicative goal
- Balancing strong emotions (negative or positive) to aid memorability

## Character‑Oriented Design

- Treat data elements as “characters” (icons, icons, metaphors) to anchor story
- Persistent characters help audience track the main message across slides
- Conflicts between characters (e.g., productivity vs. wages) create narrative tension
- Through‑line (theme) runs from start to finish, linking sub‑topics

## Takeaways for Final Projects

- Define a clear through‑line before selecting visual elements
- Choose a genre that matches desired interactivity level
- Use consistent visual cues (color, layout) to guide audience attention
- Leverage data characters and conflict to make the story compelling and memorable
