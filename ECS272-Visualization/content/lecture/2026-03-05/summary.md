## Action Items

- Ask a friend or peer to review your visualization prototype and give quick feedback.
- If your visualization is interactive, run a short usability test: let users try it with minimal instruction and observe their behavior.
- Conduct a brief pre‑study (one‑week sprint) before final implementation to catch major usability issues.
- Use a monochrome color scale (single hue with varying saturation) for categorical percentage data.
- Record user sessions (video or screen capture) to later link observed actions with post‑task questionnaire responses.
- Prepare a short post‑task interview or written recall to capture users’ thought processes.

## Evaluation Overview

- Evaluation can target design, interaction, or the whole system depending on the most critical component.
- Scope must be narrowed for practical evaluation; broad considerations are useful for planning but not for execution.
- Multiple evaluation stages are common—early (formative) and late (summative) assessments.

## Visualization vs. HCI Evaluation

- Visualization evaluation focuses on **effectiveness**: can users extract intended insights?
- HCI evaluation emphasizes **usability** and overall user experience with the interface.
- Target audience matters: design for specific users (e.g., iPhone vs. MacBook) and tailor evaluation accordingly.

## Qualitative vs. Quantitative Evaluation

- **Qualitative**: explores user behavior, reasoning, and subjective impressions; often uses think‑aloud, interviews, or observations.
- **Quantitative**: measures performance metrics (time, accuracy, error rate); yields numeric results for comparison.
- Both can be combined for a richer picture of usability and effectiveness.

## User Study Approaches

- **Case Study**: descriptive, often easier to conduct; shows how users interact in real contexts.
- **User Study**: controlled experiments with recruited participants; provides performance data.
- Modern expectations in publications favor including both to strengthen evidence.

## Practical Tips for Your Project

- Define the goal of the visualization: what insight should it convey?
- Identify the primary audience and usage environment early.
- For multi‑view visualizations, decide whether to evaluate each view separately or the whole workflow.
- Observe not only actions but also ask “why” after tasks to uncover decision logic.
- Use video recordings to sync observed behavior with questionnaire answers.

## Evaluation Methods Catalog

- **Early‑stage prototype testing**: informal user observation, expert feedback, quick iterations.
- **Theoretical analysis**: algorithmic complexity, benchmark performance (quantitative).
- **Controlled experiments**: specify tasks, measure time/accuracy, compare against baseline designs.
- **Field deployment**: real‑world integration; high effort, requires domain expert collaboration.
- **Subjective assessment**: post‑use surveys on novelty, satisfaction, perceived usefulness; timing of survey matters.

## Case Study vs. User Study Decision

- Case studies are less resource‑intensive; useful for exploratory insights.
- User studies demand recruitment, protocol design, and statistical analysis but yield stronger claims of effectiveness.
- Combining both (case study for context, user study for performance) makes the strongest argument.

## Field Deployment Challenges

- Institutional approvals, workflow integration, and training can be major hurdles.
- Often impractical for student projects; consider simulated environments or collaborations with domain experts.

## Subjective Assessment Considerations

- Capture first‑impression novelty responses, then repeat after a delay for more stable opinions.
- Tailor questions to the audience and timing of the assessment.
