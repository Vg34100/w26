# ECS272 VISUAL - Intro to Information Visualization Class

## Course Overview

- Early‑morning class (now 9 am) after schedule change
- Instructor provides syllabus overview, reading assignments, and project expectations
- No required textbook; chapters assigned from a recommended book
- Weekly readings with short write‑ups; 3 programming homeworks (\~2 weeks each)
- 2 in‑class workshops led by Yinching; attendance required for workshops and team presentations
- Final team project (3–4 members) with video presentation (8‑10 min) and written report
- Friday essay due the day after the last lecture

## Lecture Content – Core Ideas

- Visualization serves two roles: **discovery** (exploring data) and **communication** (presenting findings)
- Treat visualization as a **verb**: interactive, dynamic tools rather than static charts
- Design focus on **user interaction**: enable probing, questioning, and reasoning within the visual
- Visualization complements statistics/ML: helps validate, understand, and explain patterns
- Historical context: born from high‑performance scientific computing (late 1980s) → now a practical tool for big data, AI, and many domains

## Assignments & Projects

- **HW1**: simple setup to get familiar with programming environment (JavaScript, D3)
- **HW2 & HW3**: build web‑based interactive visualizations for chosen datasets
- **Team final project**: design and implement a multi‑view interactive application; aim for general usability, not just personal use
- **Presentation schedule**: early homework demos during lecture; final project presented via video (all team members speak)
