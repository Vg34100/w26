## Lecture Overview

- 3‑D world visualized on 2‑D displays (screens, paper, phones)
- Goal: convey multivariate data within limited dimensions
- Emphasis on choosing the right visual space for the story

## Multidimensional Encoding

- Position = primary channel (most effective)
- Add color, shape, size, etc., to encode extra dimensions
- Can represent 4–5 dimensions on a 2‑D display with careful channel use

## Visual Space & Design Choices

- Identify key relationships to highlight in the data
- Choose an optimal visual space (geographical, abstract, etc.)
- Distortion may be intentional to improve clarity or emphasize aspects

## Example: Historical Army Movement

- Uses position, width, and color to show army size, direction, and progress
- Combines geographic map with time annotations (months)
- Highlights shrinkage of forces, retreats, and major battles
- Demonstrates how visual encoding tells a complete narrative

## Scale & Axis Decisions

- Linear vs. logarithmic scales affect perception of growth rates
- Log scale useful for data with large dynamic range
- Changing axis range can emphasize or downplay differences

## Distortion & Map Design

- Maps often sacrifice geographic accuracy for readability (e.g., subway maps)
- Design decisions: straight lines, limited hue palette, clear direction cues
- Different projections (cylindrical, equal‑area, conformal) chosen based on priority (distance, area, shape)

## Color & Hue Usage

- Limited colors must be allocated to distinct categories (e.g., subway lines)
- Hue selection balances distinguishability and visual harmony
- Over‑reliance on color can mislead if not paired with other cues

## Political & Ecological Visualizations

- Election maps resized by campaign spending to reveal influence patterns
- World maps distorted to reflect population rather than land area
- Ecological footprint charts compare per‑capita consumption across countries

## Time as a Dimension

- Time can be encoded via animation, sequential ordering, or axis labeling
- Important to treat time consistently with other dimensions for coherent stories

## Take‑aways for Your Projects

- Start with the story you want to tell, then pick the visual space that best supports it
- Use position first, then add secondary channels sparingly
- Be transparent about any distortions or scaling choices to avoid misleading viewers
- Test multiple designs to see which conveys the intended insight most clearly.
