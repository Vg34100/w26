## ABM Lecture Highlights

- ABM = bottom‑up modeling of individual agents → emergent system patterns
- Core components: agents, behavioral rules, interactions, emergent outcomes
- Schelling model shows mild same‑type preference → strong segregation
- Real‑world uses: population control, economics, social‑media influence, epidemics

## Discussion Questions & Takeaways

- **Predicting polarization:** ABM can simulate scenarios but not guarantee predictions
- **Policymaker reliance:** ABM useful for stress‑testing, boundary cases, not sole decision tool
- **Model limitations:** parameter selection, validation difficulty, unrealistic assumptions, lack of ground truth

## Philosophical & Methodological Reflections

- Simple rules can generate complex emergent behavior (e.g., invisible hand, Sugarscape)
- Distinguish **complex** (simple components + interactions) vs. **complicated** (many parts, no emergent insight)
- Goal of modeling: uncover simple underlying rules beneath apparent complexity

## Technical Tools & Practices

- NetLogo recommended for introductory ABM; Python (Mesa) for advanced work
- Keep models **KISS** – “keep it simple and stupid”
- Validate by checking agent survival, long‑run stability, and reproducibility
- Incorporate randomness deliberately; understand deterministic vs. stochastic elements
