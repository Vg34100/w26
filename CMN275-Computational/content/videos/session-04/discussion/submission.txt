Links to an external site.. The general task is:

    Analyze the overall expression of sentiment, emotions, and a trait of your choice in the first speech.
    Do the same for the second speech.
    Compare both results.
    Do the same for another LLM.

Report the following outputs:

1) Show us (copy-paste, screenshot, or upload) both comparison tables from both LLMs. Study differences among the 7 evaluations. What emotional sentiment differences do the LLMs detect? Highlight agreements and eventual disagreements.

2) Take a critical look at the speeches yourself (with your own eyes!) and reflect on them. Is one of the two LLMs closer to your impression of the speeches? Can you identify why there might be differences?

3) Now imagine you (and a few friends) were put in charge of assigning sentiment and emotion scores to natural-language texts by hand (...no genAI! ...just imagine... so pre-2023...). Your job is to produce scores that are "good enough" to be used in a research study. Reflect on the following questions. Then record a short video where you tell us about your reflections:

   3.1) What exactly would you focus on in your scoring: the author's emotion, the emotion expressed in the words, or the emotion you feel as a reader? Why?

   3.2) What counts as the "thing” you score: the whole text, each paragraph, each sentence, or something else? How do you get your final score? Why?

   3.3) What labels or scoring scales would you use to quantify sentiment and emotions, and what would “mixed” or “neutral” mean?

   3.4) What would you do with sarcasm, jokes, quotes, emojis, slang, or cultural references?

   3.5) How would you handle texts that are unclear, ambiguous (sarcasm, jokes, emojis, slang, etc), or depend on context you don’t have?

   3.6) If you and your friends, who also code, disagree, what would you do? Who decides, and on what basis?

   3.7) How and at what point would you know that your system is working well enough to trust the results?

   3.8) What part of this content coding job feels most uncertain or surprisingly difficult? Any ideas what you could do about it?
