# CMN SESSION 5 - NLP

## Moral Narrative Analyzer (MoNA) Project

- Captures global news every 15 minutes, 24/7
- Uses big‑data pipeline to mine moral frames from text
- Builds “moral dictionaries” by aggregating human‑highlighted words
- Maps how moral framing changes over time and across languages
- Aims to predict real‑world events (protests, policy shifts) from moral framing

## Moral Frames & Predictive Power

- Moral conflicts (fairness, harm, loyalty, authority, sanctity) make messages engaging
- News frames can trigger subsequent events; reporting can influence behavior
- Spikes in themes (terror, immigration, cyber‑attack) line up with real incidents (e.g., Brussels attack, Brexit, US election)
- Hypothesis: unfairness cues → moral outrage → protests; can forecast when/where protests arise

## Moral Dictionaries & Text Mining

- Human coders highlight morally salient words; algorithm counts frequencies per article
- Dictionaries link words to moral categories (e.g., “frog” → fairness/cheating)
- Enables large‑scale moral profiling of news, scripts, social media, etc.

## Applications to Film Scripts & Storytelling

- MoNA applied to movie scripts (e.g., *Star Wars V*) to map character networks and sentiment arcs
- Moral conflicts identified as predictors of movie quality and box‑office success
- Tool helps studios screen hundreds of scripts quickly, flagging high‑conflict, morally rich stories

## Brain Imaging of Moral Processing

- fMRI scans while participants read moral violations (e.g., disobeying curfew)
- Machine‑learning classifier trained on brain patterns → predicts stimulus from activity
- Key regions: precuneus, medial prefrontal cortex, insula, temporal pole
- Binding moral domains (authority, loyalty, purity) activate different networks than individualizing domains (care, fairness)

## Political Ideology & Moral Foundations

- Conservatives emphasize binding domains (group cohesion, tradition, purity)
- Liberals prioritize individualizing domains (care, fairness)
- Neural activation patterns reflect these value differences, not innate brain “types”

## Early Computational Dialogue Systems

- 1980s: natural‑language interface to SQL databases (typed input → text‑to‑speech output)
- Speech recognition too slow; text‑to‑speech used first
- Voice search later generated massive spoken‑language datasets, boosting speech‑recognition accuracy

## Ethics, Responsibility, and Tech Misuse

- Researchers must anticipate ethical risks, but cannot guarantee complete prevention of misuse
- Example: Facebook/Twitter delayed response to platform misuse for propaganda; felt industry shame
- Calls for faster, transparent action and more human oversight when issues surface

## Storytelling Agents for Children

- Collaboration (UCSC + UCD) builds animated agents that interact with 5‑8‑year‑olds
- Agents model child’s ethnicity/gender, tell stories, answer questions, boost oral language and narrative comprehension

## Computational Social Science & Literary Analysis

- NLP transforms qualitative literature (e.g., Jane Austen) into quantitative data (character networks, personality cues)
- Enables new insights into narrative structure, social dynamics, and cultural storytelling patterns
- Still “foreign” to many literary scholars; needs interdisciplinary researchers comfortable with both methods

## Future Directions & Open Questions

- Extend MoNA beyond news to political speeches, books, other corpora
- Combine moral framing with brain‑based predictors for richer behavioral forecasts
- Develop “new generation” of literary theorists open to computational tools
- Explore how moral framing can be tailored to different ideological audiences for less polarized communication
