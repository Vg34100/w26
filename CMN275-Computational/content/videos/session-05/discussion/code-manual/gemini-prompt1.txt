## Phase 1: Input Analysis (Chain of Thought)

1.
**Core Constructs**: The primary construct is **Linguistic Groundedness**, measured via the **Touch Grass Ratio (TGR)**. Sub-dimensions include **Sensorimotor Strength** (perceptual/action intensity), **Ladder of Abstraction** (specificity vs. generality), and **Structural Transparency** (absence of nominalization and jargon).


2. **Boundaries**:
*
**In-Scope**: Analysis of nouns, verbs, and adjectives for their sensory referents and syntactic structure.


*
**Out-of-Scope**: Purely emotional sentiment, factual accuracy (truth-telling), or the speaker's physical location at the time of writing.




3. **Measurability**:
*
**Manifest**: Concrete nouns, specific brand names, and active verbs (e.g., "lime green Nikes," "write several drafts").


*
**Latent**: The degree of "hyperreality" or "social detachment" signaled by niche jargon density.

## Phase 2: Expanded Codebook Structure

### Section 2: Variable Definitions (Continued)

#### Variable 3: LADDER_OF_ABSTRACTION_LEVEL

*
**Variable Name**: `LADDER_OF_ABSTRACTION_LEVEL`


*
**Type**: Ordinal (1 to 8)


*
**Manifest vs. Latent**: Manifest; based on the specificity of the noun/entity.


* **Operational Definition**: Classifies the level of specificity of the text's primary referents. Level 8 represents broad, general categories with low sensory detail; Level 1 represents maximized sensory and physical detail.


*
**Numeric Mapping**:


* **8**: Broadest category (e.g., "Food").
* **5**: Specific object class (e.g., "Ice cream").
* **1**: Maximized sensory/physical detail (e.g., "A double-scoop waffle cone of Chunky Monkey").


* **Anchor Examples**:
*
**High Abstraction (Level 8)**: "Americans must protect our freedoms".


*
**Moderate Abstraction (Level 5)**: "The items will arrive at your location".


*
**Low Abstraction (Level 2)**: "The lime green Nikes will be at your door".


*
**Lowest Abstraction (Level 1)**: "Write several drafts of each paper; review notes weekly".





#### Variable 4: JARGON_DENSITY

*
**Variable Name**: `JARGON_DENSITY`


*
**Type**: Ordinal (0 to 5)


*
**Manifest vs. Latent**: Latent; requires identifying if vocabulary is industry-specific or niche to a closed digital loop.


*
**Operational Definition**: Measures the concentration of specialized vocabulary that signals group membership rather than describing a shared physical reality.


* **Numeric Mapping**:
*
**0**: General, grounded English easily understood by a layperson.


* **3**: Moderate use of technical terms requiring some domain knowledge.
*
**5**: High density of "toxic" or "obsessive" niche knowledge.




* **Anchor Examples**:
*
**High (5)**: "About the security partial trust allow list".


*
**Low/Null (0)**: "Voters must protest warrantless wiretapping".


*
**Edge Case (2)**: "The Milton Model uses nominalizations". (Scored 2 because while "Milton Model" is jargon, the sentence explains its function).


*
**Counter-Example (0)**: "He gave a tip". (Fails because it describes a common, grounded action verb).

### Section 3: Decision Logic & Ambiguity

*
**Priority Rules**: If a word is both a nominalization and a jargon term, prioritize coding it under **STRUCTURAL_DETACHMENT**.


*
**Co-Coding Rules**: A single unit can score high on both variables (e.g., a "detached" corporate strategy).


*
**Ambiguity Protocol**: Default to 0 (Not Present) if a sensory referent is unclear. Use the "Wheelbarrow Test": if you cannot put the concept in a wheelbarrow, it is likely abstract.



### Section 4: Coding Workflow

1. Read the utterance.


2. Apply the **Wheelbarrow Test**: Can the main subject be physically moved?


3. Identify **Sensorimotor** keywords (Lancaster 11) and assign **SENSORIMOTOR_STRENGTH**.


4. Identify suffixes (-ion, -ment, etc.) to assign **STRUCTURAL_DETACHMENT**.


5. Check for niche/industry terms to adjust for **Jargon Density**.


6. Generate JSON output.
