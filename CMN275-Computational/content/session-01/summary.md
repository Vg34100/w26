## Digital Revolution & Information Growth

- Late 1980s: &lt; 1 % of stored info was digital.
- 2000‑2002: digital info surpassed analog for the first time.
- 2014 estimate: \~5 zettabytes (5 × 10²¹ bytes) stored globally.
- Digital share now &gt; 99 % of all stored information.
- Information volume doubles roughly every 2–3 years.

## Scale of Digital Storage

- 5 ZB of data would form a stack reaching the Sun — ≈ 4,500 Sun‑to‑Earth piles.
- Driving a car at 130 km/h nonstop would take \~130 years to travel that distance.
- Each doubling adds as much new data as humanity has ever stored.

## DNA vs. Digital Information

- All human cells together store roughly 5 times less info than today’s digital storage.
- DNA growth ≈ 1–2 % per year, versus 25–30 % annual growth for digital data.

## Major Transitions & Emergence

- Evolutionary “major transitions” occur when a new way of processing information appears.
- Digital tech is viewed as the latest major transition in human evolution.
- Emergent properties: whole &gt; sum of parts (e.g., ant colonies, societies).

## Computational Social Science: Scope & Methods

- Applies the scientific method using computational tools.
- Covers empirical data collection, analytical modeling, and theoretical simulation.
- Core techniques: web scraping, network analysis, machine learning, NLP.

## Data, Digital Footprints & Big Data

- Digital footprints (social media, phone traces) act as real‑time observational data.
- Footprints are “data, not a foot” – they approximate behavior but aren’t perfect.
- Big data enables pattern detection beyond human manual analysis.

## Social Networks & Emergent Phenomena

- Society is a network of individuals; network structure shapes diffusion of ideas, diseases, innovations.
- Example: targeting a single infected class can curb an outbreak as effectively as closing an entire school.
- Social influence can spread contagiously (e.g., happiness across friends of friends).

## Applications & Policy Examples

- Online dating algorithms now mediate \~30–50 % of partner choices.
- Digital tools inform policy (e.g., Caribbean digital‑access plans, UN development strategies).
- Simulations help test interventions before real‑world rollout (e.g., traffic, disease control).

## Scientific Method & Modeling Approaches

- Three problem types: simplicity (few variables), disorganized complexity (averages), organized complexity (interrelated many variables).
- Induction: derive theory from data.
- Deduction: test theory against data.
- Verbal deduction: infer hypotheses from logical relationships in statements.

## Challenges: Nonlinearity, Replication Crisis, Ethics

- Social systems are highly non‑linear; linear extrapolation often fails.
- Replication crisis: many social‑science findings cannot be reproduced.
- Ethical vigilance needed: avoid harm, respect privacy, acknowledge model limitations.
